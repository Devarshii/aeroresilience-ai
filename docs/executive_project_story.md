# AeroResilience AI
## From Ground Stop to Network Recovery

**An AI-powered Airline Operations Intelligence and Recovery Decision-Support Research Platform**

### Executive Summary

Airline disruptions rarely end when the ground stop is lifted. Aircraft are out of position, flight schedules are disrupted, crews face availability constraints, and passengers risk missing connections.

AeroResilience AI investigates how airlines can make more informed recovery decisions after major flight disruptions.

Motivated by the November 2026 U.S. ground-stop incident, the platform combines historical U.S. Bureau of Transportation Statistics flight data, predictive analytics, operations research, and scenario simulation to study airline recovery strategies.

### The Solution

AeroResilience AI connects six analytical capabilities:

1. **Flight Disruption Intelligence:** Identify historical disruption patterns and prioritize flights based on modeled risk.
2. **Aircraft & Crew Recovery Optimization:** Compare departure-recovery strategies and evaluate aircraft rotation and synthetic crew constraints.
3. **Passenger Recovery Intelligence:** Analyze missed connections and simulate alternative-flight rebooking.
4. **Financial Impact Analytics:** Evaluate recovery costs using illustrative operational and passenger-related cost assumptions.
5. **Resilience & What-If Simulation:** Test how outcomes change under different operational and financial conditions.
6. **Executive Decision Command Center:** Bring the findings together in an interactive Streamlit dashboard.

### Key Findings

In a hypothetical Chicago O'Hare ground-stop experiment using historical flight schedules:

- Aircraft-aware recovery reduced modeled incremental network delay by **7.35%** compared with First-Come-First-Served scheduling.
- This represented **7,618 fewer simulated network-delay minutes**.
- The fairness-oriented policy produced the fewest delayed flights and downstream delayed flights among the four tested strategies.
- Passenger recovery and financial analysis demonstrated additional considerations beyond minimizing aircraft delay.

### Business Value

The project illustrates a decision-support approach through which airlines could compare recovery strategies based on network delay, operational constraints, passenger disruption, and illustrative recovery costs.

Rather than assuming one policy is always optimal, AeroResilience AI highlights the trade-offs that airline decision-makers must evaluate.

### Research Scope

The project uses real historical BTS flight records, but its ground-stop experiments, recovery schedules, synthetic passenger and crew information, and cost assumptions are simulated.

The November 2026 incident provides real-world motivation; the reported model improvements are not actual outcomes from that incident.

**AeroResilience AI demonstrates how analytics can help move airline operations from reactive disruption management toward informed recovery planning.**
